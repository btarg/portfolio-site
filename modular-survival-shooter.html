Project Overview

This Unity survival shooter prototype explores reusable gameplay systems for weapons, loot, and enemy encounters. I initially planned a Noita-inspired weapon builder, but reduced the scope to swappable attachments and predefined weapons that fit the level better.

Weapon components store their data while a central Weapon Holder script owns the logic. Keeping the logic in one place made the flow easier to follow while still allowing attachments to change weapon stats and handling.

Loot and Interactions

I built a reusable Interactable system for loot chests, item pickups, money, and health. This made it quick to create new pickup prefabs and place chests in the scene.

I simplified the original plan for procedurally generated component drops into manually placed chests with predefined contents and prices. This avoided a complex weapon-building interface and made balancing the level more manageable.

Difficulty and Enemy Spawning

I created difficulty presets and a linked time-of-day system using Scriptable Objects. This kept the settings easy to edit and let the visual day and night cycle reflect the selected difficulty.

After reading the Risk of Rain 2 wiki, I implemented a simplified version of its enemy-spawning system. The system increases difficulty over time and introduces higher-level enemies without manually scripting every spawn point and encounter.

What I Learned

The project reinforced the value of reducing scope early. Some systems became too modular for the problem they solved, such as using Scriptable Conditions where a simple minimum-money field on an Interactable would have been clearer. I would also improve the loot tables with weighted drops and spend more time refining enemy behaviours.
